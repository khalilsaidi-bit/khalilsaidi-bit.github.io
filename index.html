SafeGuard IIoT

Semester project at École Polytechnique de Sousse, with Wassim Bergaoui, supervised by Mr. Khaled Ben Khlifa

Fire, heat and air-quality monitoring for a workshop, with an automatic ventilation flap and remote supervision in the cloud.

Demo on the real prototype, 1 min 34 with French captions: start-up, telemetry, gas, temperature and fire alerts, remote control.

What it does

  • An ESP32 reads an IR flame sensor, a DHT11 for temperature and humidity, and an MQ-135 gas sensor.
  • A state machine on the node decides between fire, heat alert, air-quality alert and normal, and drives a local alarm and a servo-driven ventilation flap.
  • Safety never depends on the network: if Wi-Fi or the broker goes down, detection and alarm keep running.
  • Telemetry and alerts travel over MQTT to a Mosquitto broker in Docker; a Python gateway stores them in Firebase Realtime Database.
  • A Streamlit dashboard shows live values, trends and the alert log, and sends commands back to the flap.

Security

  • No anonymous access to the broker, one account per client, hashed passwords.
  • Credentials kept out of the code, closed Firebase rules, HTTPS only.

Research master's thesisISSAT Sousse, LATIS laboratory (ENISO)Feb 2026 – presentsupervised by Mr. Faycel Jellali

Grid-forming control of a three-level T-type inverter

As inverter-based sources replace rotating machines, microgrids lose the inertia that used to hold their voltage and frequency. My thesis asks whether droop control with filtered powers lets a 10 kVA, 400 V three-level T-type inverter, connected through an LCL filter, form a clean voltage on its own, couple smoothly to a weak grid (short-circuit ratio 3) and stay stable through setpoint changes and grid disturbances.

  • Full control chain in MATLAB/Simulink, sampled at 25 µs: P–ω / Q–V droop equivalent to a virtual synchronous machine (H ≈ 0.8 s), cascaded PI voltage and current loops in the dq frame, three-level SVPWM at 40 kHz with neutral-point balancing.
  • Pre-synchronisation and synchrocheck for grid coupling, and three protections: a 30 A current limiter, a threshold-based virtual impedance and a ±9.5 kW active-power limiter.
  • 21 simulation test cases, all run with the final control: islanded, coupling and islanding, grid-connected.
  • Synchronism kept without a PLL through 20° and 60° phase jumps and 150 ms three-phase and single-phase faults; unintentional islanding ridden through without a mode change.
1.8 %voltage THD in islanded mode (1.0 % for current)
±8.7°phase error at breaker closing, coupling current below 80 % of rated peak
< 0.2 sfor active power to reach its setpoint, with no overshoot
1.1 %at the coupling point for a 10 % grid voltage sag

Results from simulation only, with an ideal DC bus and lossless switches; the thesis sets out the limits and the path to hardware-in-the-loop and a reduced-power prototype.

Automation and supervision of an HV transformer cooling system

In a combined-cycle power plant, the ODAF cooling (oil directed, air forced) of a power transformer was still driven by hard-wired relay logic: thresholds were hard to change, nothing reached the control room, and operators had to work close to high voltage. We replaced it with a Siemens S7-1200 solution designed in TIA Portal.

  • CPU 1214C with an RTD module for PT100 oil temperature probes, a pressure transmitter and an oil-level float.
  • A PID_Compact controller in the cyclic interrupt OB30 sets the speed of the oil pumps and fans continuously, through an SM 1232 analog output module and variable-speed drives, instead of on/off staging.
  • Modular program called from OB1: a scaling function converts the raw 0–27648 readings into °C and bar (NORM_X, SCALE_X), a safety function stops everything on emergency stop, oil level or a pressure outside 0.5–3.5 bar, then separate blocks for automatic and manual modes, with a shared data block.
  • The temperature setpoint comes from the HMI, and the PID is held in reset whenever automatic mode is off, so it can never drive the outputs during manual maintenance.
  • Operating modes modelled with GEMMA and Grafcets: safety, supervision, normal production and manual control.
  • KTP700 Basic touch panel built in WinCC: home, automatic and manual views, setpoint, oil temperature gauge, pump and fan status, fault reset.
  • Validated in simulation with S7-PLCSIM and the WinCC runtime simulator.

TurtleBot 3 control in simulation with ROS 2 Humble and Gazebo

A mobile robot perceives, decides and acts through separate programs that must exchange data continuously. I set up a ROS 2 Humble environment from scratch on an Ubuntu 22.04 virtual machine, studied how a simulated TurtleBot 3 Burger is wired in ROS 2, then wrote my own Python package to drive it: one node follows a square in open loop, the other avoids obstacles from its LiDAR.

  • Mapped the robot's ROS 2 graph: who publishes and subscribes to /scan (LaserScan), /odom (Odometry) and /cmd_vel (Twist). The LiDAR returns 360 rays at 1° resolution, 0.12 to 3.5 m, at about 4.5 Hz in the VM.
  • Drove the robot directly in velocity and checked the circular-path model R = v/ω: 0.1 m/s and 0.5 rad/s give R = 0.2 m.
  • Package tp_turtlebot (ament_python, built with colcon), node carre: a 1 m square in sixteen steps, driven by a 10 Hz timer on simulation time. Odometry measured an 8.4 cm closure error; I traced it to angle discretisation (88.8° per turn instead of 90°), inertia and wheel slip, and proposed a closed-loop version.
  • Node stop_obstacle: a reactive avoidance behaviour that watches the minimum distance over a ±15° front cone rather than a single ray, since the robot is about 14 cm wide.
  • Invalid readings (inf, nan, 0) are treated as a clear path; the robot turns below 0.40 m and only moves on beyond 0.50 m, a hysteresis that stops it from hesitating at the threshold. It subscribes with the sensor-data QoS profile.
  • Analysed the limits: at 0.15 m/s the robot travels about 3 cm between two scans, and it always turns the same way; choosing the freer side would make avoidance faster.

Hybrid Marine Platform: wave and tidal energy on one axis

Offshore projects cut costs by sharing one foundation, mooring and cable between several converters. We applied that idea at student scale: a one-metre floating prototype carries a Faraday-lamp wave generator at the surface and a small tidal turbine near the seabed, each with its own circuit, on a single float, axis and ballast. The structure is made of PVC tubing and an extensible siphon that replaces a gimbal and a slide, and the purchased parts cost 111.5 TND. We are writing it up as an IEEE-format paper.

  • We designed the generator: a 3D-printed PETG tube in four parts, a 1,500-turn copper coil and an NdFeB magnet levitating between repelling end magnets instead of springs.
  • We designed a 22 × 46 mm double-sided PCB that slides into the lamp head: Schottky bridge, 0.22 F supercapacitor, switch and LED.
  • Electromagnetic model in Python (SciPy, elliptic integrals): coupling of 9.15 V/(m/s), magnetic-spring natural frequency of 5.5 Hz, so the generator mostly responds to shocks and chop rather than slow swell.
  • Tidal turbine model: 12 to 94 mW captured between 0.5 and 1 m/s. These are theoretical values; pool tests will calibrate the model.

Course project, dependabilityÉcole Polytechnique de Soussewith Wassim Bergaouisupervised by Prof. F. Ben Hmida

Multi-sensor monitoring of an electric pump

Can the detection index D of an FMEA be measured instead of guessed? Using the public SKAB dataset (34 runs on a real pump test bench, eight sensors at 1 Hz, seven failure modes), we built a fault detector in Python and judged it the way a maintenance team would: is the first alarm right, how fast does it come, how often does it ring for nothing.

  • Hotelling T² on short-term deviation features, with a Ledoit–Wolf covariance estimate; it beat Isolation Forest in six cases out of seven.
  • Vibration and hydraulic sensors together caught 82% of faults on the first alarm. Motor current, measured as 1 Hz RMS, caught almost nothing.
  • A model learned at one operating point failed at another (82% fell to 26%): the reference must be relearned after every setting change.
  • With four sensors, the criticality of every failure mode in the pump's FMEA dropped by 25 to 75%.
